# Interpretation Contract

## Working English Version

Public methodological notes on the interpretation boundaries of NeoMundi runtime governance signals, metrics, and decisions.

This repository documents the interpretation contract applicable to signals produced or exposed within the NeoMundi framework.

It does not disclose proprietary formulas, production coefficients, internal weights, thresholds, full decision rules, or runtime inference mechanisms used in the NeoMundi engine.

## Objective

The objective of this repository is to clarify how NeoMundi signals may be interpreted within a runtime governance framework.

It aims to clearly distinguish between:

- a signal;
- a metric;
- a governance decision;
- proof;
- human or business responsibility;
- contextual interpretation.

The interpretation contract does not seek to impose a universal reading of AI systems.

It provides a minimal framework to avoid confusion, overinterpretation, and misuse of signals.

## Core Principle

A signal is not truth.

A metric is not a decision.

A governance decision is not automatic legal proof.

A score does not replace human, business, or regulatory analysis.

NeoMundi signals should be understood as runtime observability and governance artifacts.

They help interpret the state of an AI generation or execution segment, but they do not claim to produce absolute truth.

## Signal

A signal is observable information produced during or about an AI execution.

Examples:

- G;
- ΔG;
- coherence;
- informational density;
- informational energy;
- ESI.

A signal may help identify stability, variation, fragility, transition, or an area requiring attention.

A signal does not, by itself, constitute a definitive conclusion.

## Metric

A metric is a structured measure or an indicator calculated from observable variables.

It may be descriptive, derived, or composite.

A metric helps organize observation.

It should not be confused with proof, truth, or an automatic decision.

## Governance Decision

A governance decision corresponds to an operational state produced from one or more signals, metrics, or configured rules.

Examples:

- ALLOW;
- FLAG;
- BLOCK;
- SIGNAL;
- REVIEW;
- ESCALATE.

A governance decision does not necessarily mean that the response is true, false, dangerous, or compliant.

It means that a system applies an action policy based on observed signals and defined thresholds.

## Proof

Proof implies a higher level of anchoring, verification, traceability, or validation than a simple runtime signal.

A signal may contribute to a chain of proof.

However, a runtime signal taken in isolation does not constitute complete legal, scientific, or factual proof.

## Responsibility for Interpretation

Final interpretation always depends on the usage context.

It may belong to:

- the user;
- the human operator;
- the orchestration system;
- the compliance officer;
- the auditor;
- the application provider;
- the applicable regulatory framework.

NeoMundi provides runtime governance signals.

The use, configuration, thresholds, action policies, and final decisions belong to the system or organization that operates them.

## Contextual Interpretation

The same signal may have different meanings depending on:

- the application domain;
- the risk level;
- the task type;
- the model used;
- the business context;
- the configured thresholds;
- whether a human is in the loop;
- regulatory requirements;
- the expected level of proof.

A low score does not automatically prove an error.

A high score does not guarantee absolute truth.

A FLAG does not necessarily mean that the response is false.

An ALLOW does not mean that the response is perfect.

A BLOCK does not mean that the response is universally dangerous.

## Non-Claims

The interpretation contract specifies that NeoMundi signals do not claim to provide:

- absolute semantic truth;
- universal factual certainty;
- reconstruction of the model’s internal cognition;
- a guarantee of absence of hallucination;
- automatic legal certification;
- a moral decision;
- complete regulatory compliance by themselves;
- a substitute for human responsibility.

## Relationship with Interoperability

The interoperability contract defines how signals may circulate between several technical or organizational layers.

The interpretation contract defines what these signals may mean, what they do not mean, and which responsibilities remain attached to their use.

Interoperability: how layers communicate.

Interpretation: how signals should be read.

## Methodological Position

The interpretation contract aims to make NeoMundi signals readable, auditable, and usable without transforming them into absolute truths.

It protects:

- methodological rigor;
- the end user;
- the auditor;
- the integrator;
- the application provider;
- human responsibility;
- and the proprietary core of the NeoMundi engine.

## Limits

This repository provides a public and methodological definition.

It does not document internal production parameters.

It does not constitute complete documentation of the NeoMundi engine.

It does not replace a technical, legal, scientific, or regulatory audit.

It serves as a common basis for interpreting signals within a runtime governance framework.
